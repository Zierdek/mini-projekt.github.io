A small Volvo with big character

The Volvo C30 debuted in 2006 and was produced until 2013 in Ghent, Belgium. It was the smallest model in the Swedish brand’s lineup at the time, and one of its boldest in terms of styling. Instead of a practical estate, Volvo went for a compact three-door hatchback with a sporty profile. The C30 was meant to appeal to people who value style over space. Technically it was based on a platform shared with the second-generation Ford Focus and the Mazda 3, but inside and out it was pure Volvo.

Design that stands out from the crowd

The C30’s most recognisable feature is its rear end, with a large glass tailgate. It’s a nod to the iconic P1800ES from the early 1970s. The glass gives the car a distinctive look that’s hard to mistake for any other hatchback. Inside, the eye is drawn to the four individual seats and the slim, so-called “floating” centre console. The whole thing looks modern and light, in keeping with the Scandinavian design tradition.

Engines and versions

The range of engines was wide: from economical petrol and diesel units, through DRIVe versions focused on low fuel consumption, to the sporty T5 with its turbocharged 2.5-litre engine. A fully electric version was also built in small numbers. You’ll find a detailed breakdown on the Model versions page.

Who is the C30 for?

The C30 is a good choice for someone looking for a small, stylish car with the safety Volvo is known for. There are trade-offs to keep in mind, though: the boot is small, and inside there are four seats, not five.
